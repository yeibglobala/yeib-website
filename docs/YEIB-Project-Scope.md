## 1. Project Overview

YEIB Investment Fund is a **wholesale investment fund** — "a platform that funds funders" — designed to make youth- and women-led MSMEs in Nigeria (and eventually pan-African markets) investable, by combining financial services (direct investment, intermediary capital, guaranteed bank lending) with non-financial support (reimbursable grants, ecosystem development, capacity building). The website is the fund's first public-facing surface and must do three things at once:

1. **Signal institutional credibility** to DFIs, pension funds, banks, and private-sector investors who need to trust the fund with capital.
2. **Signal accessibility and fairness** to youth- and women-led MSMEs who have been let down by systems before and need to believe access is real and unbiased.
3. **Avoid two failure modes explicitly flagged in discovery:** looking like a government initiative, and looking like a bank (regulatory sensitivity — YEIB is a *fund*, not a bank, and must never invite CBN scrutiny by reading as one). It should also avoid reading as a "startup" — anything that "screams startup is out."

The sweet spot, per discovery, sits at the **midpoint between looking like a bank and looking like a government institution** — closer to an institutional financial platform (Blackstone, JP Morgan — used only as tonal/minimal references) than to a flashy fintech or an NGO.

## 2. Brand Foundation

### Naming (strict — per Verbal Identity doc)
- Always write **YEIB Investment Fund** in full for first mentions, formal copy, investor materials, and official documents.
- **YEIB** alone is acceptable shorthand after first mention.
- YEIB always appears in **capital letters** — never lowercase, pluralized, hyphenated, or spelled out as separate initials (no "Yeib," "yeib," "YEIBs," "Y-E-I-B," "Y.E.I.B").
- Never write "YEIB Fund" — "investment" must stay in the name.
- Sub-initiatives are named as belonging to YEIB, e.g. "YEIB Credit Guarantee Fund," "YEIB Ecosystem Development Fund."
- No unofficial nicknames or abbreviations should ever be invented.

### Positioning
- **"They're a platform that funds funders."** — YEIB is a wholesale fund, not a direct-to-entrepreneur lender in most cases.
- Core purpose line: **"Funding the dreams of young people & women."**
- Ultimate goal: **make young people look investable** — YEIB exists to close the gap between "high-risk, unattractive to investors" and "bankable, proven opportunity."
- Tagline candidate from discovery: *"YEIB proves the birth of more [programs like this] was right"* — positioned as an **evidence-building initiative** for the thesis that Nigerian youth are investable.
- Target: mobilize toward a **$300M** fund target, drawing in DFIs, pension funds, and private capital.

### Target Audience (two-sided)
**Supply side (funders/investors):** DFIs, private sector investors, foundations/philanthropic capital, other capital providers.
**Demand side (beneficiaries):** Youth- and women-led MSMEs, financial institutions (commercial banks, MFBs), ecosystem players (ESOs, academia, policy), intermediaries (PE, VC, fintech).

**Demand-side eligibility (be explicit on the site):**
- Under age 35 (with a specific band for women — "young people, especially women").
- Youth-led business at the helm, OR management team that's ≥50% under 35, OR employing people under 35 — spanning ownership, management, and employment.
- Sector-agnostic, but priority sectors: **Agriculture, Trade, Creative, ICT.**

### Verbal Identity — Voice Pillars (from YEIB Brand Verbal Identity Document, TM Global)

YEIB's voice must be **clear, credible, and human**, expressed through three pillars:

**1. Confident**
- *Assured* — clear on mandate, role, commitment, point of view.
- *Factual* — every claim backed by evidence, data, market realities.
- *Thorough* — no shallow or half-way support messaging.
- **Never:** presumptuous, delusional (implying every business is already investment-ready), or dismissive of real barriers.

**2. Inspiring**
- *Optimistic* — positive language about growth and investment readiness.
- *Experienced* — draws on institutional depth in entrepreneurship and ecosystem strengthening.
- *Insightful* — names the real barriers and trust gaps rather than glossing over them.
- **Never:** pessimistic, performative ("motivational language that sounds good but says little"), or patronizing — MSMEs are **capable builders**, not helpless beneficiaries waiting to be rescued.

**3. Honest**
- *Transparent* — clear on process, criteria, results, impact; no inflated reports.
- *Fair* — explicit that criteria don't bend for tribe, gender, location, personal connections, or influence.
- **Never:** misleading (false expectations on funding/timelines), exploitative (using MSME struggles as "emotional bait"), or biased.

**Writing rules distilled from the doc:**
- Lead with what the audience needs to know/do/believe — introduce YEIB's role second.
- Use active, simple sentences: what's offered, what steps to take, what outcome to expect.
- Make every claim specific — eligibility, capital type, partnerships, process, data, outcomes — never vague.
- Use proof (verified numbers, real outcomes, named partners) instead of broad claims or emotional appeals.
- Explain *how things work*, not just that opportunities exist — show steps, requirements, review process, support structure.

**Culture/behavior words from the founding team's own brainstorm (useful as supporting texture, not headline copy):** determined, approachable, visionary, authoritative, transformative, resilient, genuine, fearless; responsibility, excellence, teamwork, truthfulness, inclusivity, discipline, loyalty, innovation.

### Content Themes to Weave In
- **Life-cycle support / "growth partner" positioning:** "Whoever you are, whatever stage you are, whatever kind of support you need, we can cater to you — we just need to work out what that looks like." Support changes based on business stage (this is a named key differentiator vs. adjacent programs like iDICE, which "captures a small subsection").
- **Two funding paths, explained plainly:**
  - *Financial services* — Direct investment (youths & women, MSMEs); Intermediaries (PE, fintech, VC — who then deploy to end recipients); Banks (commercial & MFB, with a guarantee window covering up to 75% via the FIRST Law/Credit Guarantee mechanism).
  - *Non-financial services* — Reimbursable grants (not interest-based), smaller ticket grants, grants to ESOs/entrepreneurship support centers (up to $50K), and ecosystem/infrastructure funding.
- **"Ecosystem Development," not "Capacity Building."** Discovery notes are explicit: avoid language the brand can't defend or own. Frame this work as *ecosystem development* across MSMEs, ESOs, academia, and policymakers — not as a top-down "capacity building" program. It's an ethos aimed at sustainability and bankability, not a training checkbox.
- **Trust/access framing:** There's an acknowledged trust deficit in government-anchored projects — YEIB's answer is radical transparency and equitable access ("without knowing anybody"). Consider a line of messaging like: *young people should know they were chosen deliberately* — access based on merit and clear criteria, not proximity.
- **Evidence/KPI framing for credibility:** amount disbursed, number of youth/MSMEs supported, jobs created, evidence of bankable youth-led businesses (proof at ~12 months), value proven to other investors and NPL performance on loans (proof at ~24 months). This "proof → bankability → investability → more investors join" chain is a strong narrative arc for a Transparency/Impact page.
- **Structuring investor entry points:** the site should read as a landing page for DFIs and institutional supply-side partners as much as a program page for MSMEs — both "supply" and "demand" sides need clearly defined, separate journeys/CTAs. Minimum filters should qualify visitors early (self-select into "I'm an investor/partner" vs. "I'm an entrepreneur").
- **Soft-launch framing:** early site copy can be structured around informing, collecting interest, and building a waitlist ahead of full application opening — welcoming/inviting/encouraging language, not yet a live application funnel.

## 3. Design Direction

*(To be validated against the actual Figma file — the points below come from your YEIB Design System reference and the discovery-notes brainstorm on visual tone, not from viewing the Figma frames directly.)*

### 3.1 Visual Tone (from discovery)
- **Midpoint aesthetic:** not a bank, not a government portal, not a startup. Financial-institution polish with restrained warmth.
- References explicitly called out as directionally right: **Blackstone, JP Morgan** (very minimal), **iDICE Bridge**, **Orange Corners** (loved for impact numbers + iconography), **BPI France** (loved overall — described as "enough life, not too expressive, more controlled").
- References explicitly flagged as **too far** in one direction: **a-typical ventures** (too fun/startup-coded), **the radical fund** (too interactive) — avoid this level of playfulness.
- Overall brief: **"financial institution should come through."** Traditional-leaning typography was favored by at least one stakeholder ("very traditional with fonts").

### 3.2 Color
- Base palette candidates discussed: **blue, teal, wine/maroon**, with an openness to introducing **green** (shareholder association with green) — but restrained, "play around with it," not a dominant motif.
- Source of truth for exact hex values, type scale, spacing, and components is the [YEIB Design System in Figma](https://www.figma.com/design/3ALqMMVy4OkPDG1RD03H5k/YEIB-Website?node-id=384-15&t=lP1VZz933QWWzafF-4) — this scope doc should be read alongside that file, not in place of it. Figma blocks automated/logged-out access, so I couldn't pull the exact tokens into this doc directly; once you export or screenshot the relevant frames, I can fold the precise hex codes, type sizes, and component specs in here the way I did for HDI.

### 3.3 Typography
- Traditional/institutional serif-or-structured-sans pairing is favored (per "very traditional with fonts" note) — confirm exact typefaces from your design system doc once shared.

### 3.4 Component & Layout Direction
- **Iconography and impact numbers should be a headline visual motif** (explicitly praised in Orange Corners reference) — large stat callouts, verified metrics, clear data visualization for fund allocation and impact (mirrors YEIB's own KPI/proof narrative in Section 2).
- Avoid anything that reads as "interactive/gamified" in the way "the radical fund" does — no playful startup UI patterns.
- Two distinct visitor journeys should be visually legible from the homepage: a **supply-side path** (DFIs/investors/partners) and a **demand-side path** (MSMEs/entrepreneurs) — consider a dual-CTA hero or an early fork in navigation, since discovery notes stress these are structurally different audiences with different needs and messaging.
- A dedicated **"For DFIs / Partners" landing page** was specifically mentioned as a planning need — build this as a first-class page, not an afterthought under "About."
- Lifecycle/stage-based support should be visualized somewhere (e.g., a simple stage-based diagram: idea → early-stage → growth → scale) reflecting the "growth partner" positioning.

## 4. Website Content Style Guide

- **Tone:** Institutional confidence with warmth — write like a serious capital allocator who genuinely wants more people to succeed, not like a government program or a startup pitch deck.
- **Every claim needs a receipt.** Numbers, eligibility criteria, and process steps should be specific and sourced — this is a direct requirement from the Verbal Identity doc ("use proof: verified numbers, real outcomes, credible partners... instead of broad claims").
- **Name the mechanism, not just the outcome.** E.g., don't just say "we support MSMEs" — say "direct investment, intermediary capital via PE/VC/fintech partners, and bank-guaranteed lending up to 75% loan coverage."
- **Avoid:** the words "capacity building" (use "ecosystem development" instead), anything that could read as a government program, anything overly playful/startup-coded, vague motivational language, emotional appeals that use MSME hardship as leverage.
- **Prefer:** plain descriptions of process ("here's how to apply," "here's what you need," "here's what happens next"), clearly separated audience paths, verified stats presented with source/date tags (mirroring HDI's own "Verified" pattern, which is a strong cross-project convention worth reusing here too).
- **CTAs:** action-plus-clarity, e.g. "See if you qualify," "Partner with YEIB," "Explore funding routes" — avoid generic "Get Started" language that doesn't specify for whom.
- **Regulatory care:** copy should never imply YEIB is a deposit-taking bank or lender in the CBN-regulated sense — always "fund," never "bank." This is a compliance-sensitive naming boundary raised directly in discovery, not just a style preference.

## 5. Suggested Site Structure

1. **Home** — Dual-path hero (investor/partner vs. entrepreneur), what YEIB is (wholesale fund / platform that funds funders), proof/impact stat band, two-track funding overview (financial + non-financial services), priority sectors, closing CTA.
2. **For Entrepreneurs (MSMEs)** — Eligibility (age, ownership/management/employment thresholds, sector focus), what's available (direct investment, credit guarantee routes, grants), lifecycle/stage-based support explainer, how to apply / express interest (soft-launch waitlist framing).
3. **For Investors & Partners (DFIs, banks, PE/VC, foundations)** — Fund thesis, target size ($300M), co-ownership/governance context (as much as is public), why invest through YEIB, how intermediary/guarantee mechanisms work, contact/partnership path.
4. **How YEIB Works** — The two-sided model (supply/demand), financial vs. non-financial services breakdown, ecosystem development approach (not "capacity building"), fairness/transparency commitments.
5. **Impact & Transparency** — KPI framework (disbursement amounts, MSMEs supported, jobs created, bankability evidence, NPL performance), evidence-building narrative ("proving the thesis").
6. **About** — Mission, positioning as a growth partner, life-cycle support philosophy, priority sectors (Agriculture, Trade, Creative, ICT).
7. **Contact / Get Involved** — Separate intake for entrepreneurs vs. institutional partners; waitlist/interest form for soft launch.

## 7. Tech Stack & Project Folder Structure

### 7.1 Stack

| Layer | Choice |
|---|---|
| Framework | **Next.js (latest)** — App Router |
| UI library | React (as shipped with Next.js) |
| Icons | **Lucide (react-lucide)** |
| Animation | **GSAP** (with ScrollTrigger for scroll-based reveals — stat counters, section transitions, hero motion) |
| Styling | Tailwind CSS (recommended pairing with Next.js/Lucide; confirm if a different system is preferred) |
| Fonts | Asul + Chivo + General Sans, loaded per the YEIB Design System (Fontshare / self-hosted) |

### 7.2 Folder Structure — Feature-Based + Component-Based Hybrid

Organized so that anything specific to one part of the site (a page/feature) lives together, while shared, reusable UI lives in a common component library. This keeps feature work isolated (easy to build/update "For Entrepreneurs" without touching "For Investors") while avoiding duplicated buttons, cards, and tags across the site.

**Guiding rules:**
- **`app/`** stays thin — routing and page composition only, pulling in feature components rather than defining UI inline.
- **`features/<name>/`** owns anything specific to that page — its own components and content data live together, so a feature can be built, reviewed, or handed off independently.
- **`components/`** only holds UI that's reused across two or more features (buttons, tags, cards, nav, footer) — if something is only ever used in one feature, it stays in that feature's folder, not here.
- **`animations/`** centralizes GSAP setup so animation timing/easing stays consistent across the site (e.g., the same reveal easing for stat counters wherever they appear).
- Design tokens (Evergreen, Mint Leaf, Tiger Orange, Pale Oak, Mint Cream, and the Asul/Chivo/General Sans type scale) should be defined once in `styles/globals.css` (as Tailwind theme extensions or CSS variables) and referenced everywhere — never hardcoded per component.
