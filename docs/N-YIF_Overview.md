## 1. Overview

### Context & Target Market

- The objective of the YEIB is to facilitate growth and scale of growth-oriented, dynamic, youth-led MSMEs.
- The YEIB itself is **sector agnostic**, but particularly attuned to supporting businesses to become **climate resilient, gender positive, digitally enabled** and **poised for growth through inter- and intra-regional trade**.
- The YEIB addresses key challenges and provides **both financial and non-financial support**, working actively with partners and stakeholders to concurrently develop the entrepreneurial ecosystem.

**Tagline:** The Nigeria YEIB is designed to support youth-led, growth-oriented businesses to secure the financial and non-financial support they need to grow and scale.

**Challenges addressed**

| Access to finance | Ecosystem |
|---|---|
| Availability of alternatives | Unfavorable policies |
| High cost | Siloed nature of ecosystem |
| High risk | Shallow nature of support provided to MSMEs |
| Restrictive loan terms and ticket sizes | Limited access to training and skilled labor |
| Lack of collateral | |
| Lack of data and infrastructure | |
| Limited access to funding | |

### Strategy & Distinctive Positioning

- **Initial capitalization:** US$ 300 million, with US$ 100 million injected into the YEIB via a sovereign loan from AfDB to the Federal Government of Nigeria.
- **Expected impact:** assist **38k MSMEs** and create **161k direct jobs**, while also being financially sustainable.

**Ticket sizes**

| Recipient | Ticket size |
|---|---|
| Capital for MSMEs | US$ 1K – 500K |
| Capital for VCs / PE firms | US$ 5 million |
| Technical assistance (TA) to MSMEs | up to US$ 5K |
| TA for ESOs / BDS providers | up to US$ 25K |

**Approach:** Expand financing · Develop the broader ecosystem in parallel (climate resilience, digital transformation, gender equity, positioning for inter- and intra-regional trade).

### YEIB Offering (six offerings)

1. Patient capital for local VC and PE firms
2. Direct equity and quasi-equity for MSMEs
3. First loss guarantees to financial institutions (FIs)
4. Reimbursable grants to MSMEs
5. Grants to ESOs / BDS providers
6. Funding to promote an enabling ecosystem

### Purpose & Mandate

- N-YIF is a **government-enabled, private sector-led investment fund** established under a **US$ 100 million sovereign loan from AfDB**, with a total capitalisation target of **US$ 300 million**.
- **Mandate:** catalyse inclusive growth and youth entrepreneurship in Nigeria by mobilising tailored financial and non-financial support for youth-owned MSMEs.
- **Ownership:** jointly owned by NSIA and DBN as in-country sponsors, with the **N-YIF ManCo** as the operational vehicle, designed to be commercially sustainable and mission-driven.
- **Structure:** a blended structure designed to maximise development impact and private capital mobilisation, with strong gender and climate integration throughout.
- **Governance:** Board with **60% independent directors** (chaired by an Independent Director) and a highly qualified, independently selected Investment Committee with diverse, gender-balanced expertise.

### N-YIF's Three Instruments (At a Glance)

| # | Instrument | Share of AUM | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Equity Investment Fund** | 65% | 22% direct and 43% indirect equity & quasi-equity into high-growth youth ventures, PE/VC funds and other intermediaries |
| 2 | **Credit Guarantee Fund** | 25% | Risk-sharing guarantees to unlock commercial lender and MFI lending to youth MSMEs, up to 75% coverage |
| 3 | **Ecosystem Development Fund** | 10% | Grants and reimbursable grants towards capacity building and catalytic capital to increase the number of investment-ready MSMEs |

### 20-Year Targets

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Total jobs created | ~1.6 million |
| Indirect jobs | ~1.44 million |
| Direct jobs | ~166 thousand |
| Businesses supported | ~38,400 |
| ESOs mobilised | 118 |
| Gender parity target | Year 5 |

### Ecosystem & Complementary Vehicles

| Vehicle | Description |
|---|---|
| **Next-Gen** (retail lending vehicle) | Dedicated retail working-capital loans (USD 1k–10k) for very early-stage youth businesses · simplified KYC & digital disbursement to reach informal entrepreneurs · flexible repayment (60–90-day cycles) matched to cash-flow needs · designed to plug the "last-mile" micro-credit gap unserved by wholesale DFIs · *(deck annotation: "Next-gen … didn't kickoff")* |
| **I-DICE Fund** | USD 170 million closed-end VC vehicle targeting Digital & Creative sectors · minority equity stakes in growth-stage startups (Seed – Series C) · hands-on TA and market connections for portfolio companies · fills sector-specific funding gap for high-potential tech and media enterprises |
| **DBN / ICGL** | Wholesale debt facility providing long-term loans to partner financial institutions & MFIs · Impact Credit Guarantee Ltd. (ICGL) offers 70–80% first-loss guarantees on MSME loans · on-lends at concessional rates (8–12% p.a.) to stimulate credit supply · scales working-capital and capex financing via existing financial institutions |
| **JICA / NSIA Fund** | JICA & NSIA collaboration to implement Nigeria's Startup Act · policy reforms: streamlined registration, tax incentives, IP protection · capacity-building for incubators, accelerators and regulatory bodies · lays the regulatory foundation and data platform for early-stage venture growth |
| **AFAWA Initiative** | AfDB-led Women Entrepreneurs Finance Initiative · partial credit guarantees (up to 50%) for women-led MSME lending · dedicated technical assistance and capacity-building programs · addresses gender financing gap within the broader youth entrepreneurship agenda |

### N-YEIB's Sweet Spot

- **Blended capital:** combines equity, quasi-equity, guarantees & grants
- **Youth-focused:** across all sectors & stages, with emphasis on under-served segments
- **Ecosystem development:** grants and TA to ESOs, policy convening and data platform
- **Permanent vehicle:** 20-year lifecycle vs. one-off programs

## 3. Structure and Governance

### The YEIB is a government-enabled, private sector-led investment fund

It provides a range of financial and non-financial products and services to Nigerian youth-owned businesses through **three distinct financing instruments**.

| Instrument | Share of AUM | Products and services | Rationale | Revenue stream |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Equity Investment Fund** | 65% | Capitalization of local VC, PE firms & other investment vehicles | Provide patient capital and offer tailor-made financing solutions with favorable terms to youth-led businesses across sectors | Capital gains from fund returns |
| | | Direct tailor-made investments, e.g. equity, convertible debt, RBF, in youth-led businesses | Provide early-stage capital to catalyze growth | Interest earnings & profit |
| | | First loss capital for VC and PE funds | Increase access to financing for MSMEs | Capital gains from exits |
| **2. Credit Guarantee Fund** | 25% | First loss credit guarantees to commercial lenders and MFIs, up to 75% coverage | Unlock additional capital from FIs and MFIs to increase lending to SMEs by addressing the systemic bottleneck of lack of collateral | Guarantee fees |
| **3. Ecosystem Development Fund** | 10% | Non-profit entity providing funding and technical support to promote an enabling ecosystem (e.g. research, data, policy) | Support to address ecosystem challenges constraining SME growth and development | No financial return |
| | | Grants to ESOs, BDS providers and other providers (e.g. certification agencies) | Increase number of investment-ready MSMEs · Increase quality and breadth of capacity-building services available for SMEs | No financial return |
| | | Direct reimbursable grants to MSMEs | Provide catalytic capital for very early-stage MSMEs | Grant principal returned |

### YEIB Nigeria Investment Management Company Ltd. (ManCo)

**Flow of the structure**

- **ManCo investors** (NSIA, DBN, Other Donors) inject capital to cover ManCo's operating expenses for the first few years.
- **ManCo** sits under an **Advisory Board** and a **Board of Directors**, with a **Management Team**:
  - Equity Investment Fund → **Investment Committee**
  - Ecosystem Development Fund → **Grant Committee**
- ManCo issues an **investment mandate** and a **grant mandate** to independent special-purpose financing entities and receives a **management fee (2–2.5%)**.
- The **Chief Risk Officer (CRO)** oversees coordination of services and beneficiaries, M&E, etc.

**Independent Special Purpose Financing Entities**

| Entity | Contents | Capital source |
|---|---|---|
| Equity Investment Fund | PE & VC funds · Direct investments | Fund Investors (LPs): FGN Escrow, DFIs. Capital injected for investment in funds to deploy to youth-led businesses |
| Ecosystem Development Fund | Grants | Grant Donors: FGN Escrow, Other Donors. Capital injected for grants to youth-led businesses |
| Credit Guarantee Fund | DBN's Impact Credit Guarantee Limited (ICGL) creates a ring-fenced youth business window to provide guarantees to participating financial institutions | Investors: FGN Escrow, Other Investors. Capital injected for guarantees to youth-led businesses |

**Legend:** green arrow = flow of capital · purple arrow = governance · yellow arrow = working relationship.

**Inception team**

- NSIA has appointed and is hosting an inception-stage management team, with AfDB recommendation. Operationalisation commenced in **Q3 2025**.
- The team comprises an Inception CEO, with support from AfDB, NSIA and DBN. Engagement with shareholder partners to establish critical structures, including the three financing instruments, is well underway.
- NSIA, DBN and MoFI, with AfDB support, are to appoint a Search Firm to hire a permanent ManCo Management Team to formally launch operations.

### ManCo Board of Directors and Advisory Board

Highest governing bodies, providing strategic guidance for the Nigeria YEIB.

- Independent, non-shareholding members from the private sector (entrepreneurs, investors, business development experts) form the Board's **majority**, in line with corporate best practice.
- The Boards reflect strong representation of **youth and women** and a balance of investment, technical support and policy expertise.

## 4. Operationalisation & Current Roadmap

### Key levers considered: Impact Assumptions (the 3A Framework)

*Source cited in deck: BII, OECD.*

| Lens | Guiding question | Sub-questions |
|---|---|---|
| **Impact** | Are we moving the needle on youth entrepreneurship? | What would have happened to jobs, revenues or productivity had YEIB not intervened? · Have YEIB-backed companies grown into larger, more sustainable businesses (revenue scale and longevity) compared to peers? · Are there spill-over effects in under-served regions or sectors beyond direct investees? · How many youth (and women) founders have gone on to re-invest in and strengthen the broader ecosystem? |
| **Additionality** | Are we genuinely unlocking new money & markets? | Would this enterprise have attracted capital without YEIB? · Are we truly crowding in new investors, or displacing capital that would have invested anyway? · Which local DFIs or CBN policies have shifted their risk appetite thanks to our guarantees? · Have policy and regulatory stakeholders become more engaged because YEIB demonstrated a viable youth window? |
| **Risk** | Are we balancing patient-capital upside with capital preservation? | Are we preserving capital through this investment? · Does our portfolio allocation protect us from major downsides? · Do we have the right balance for long-term patient capital and liquidity needs? · How would currency, political or guarantee-call shocks affect our break-even? · What reputational or ESG risks flow from each window (equity, guarantees, grants)? |
